# 01.AI Interactive Presentation Script

**Recommended duration:** 15–18 minutes  
**Audience:** CEOs, senior government leaders, enterprise executives and strategic partners  
**Core message:** AI transformation is not the deployment of more models. It is the redesign of how an institution understands reality, makes decisions, executes action and verifies outcomes.

Text in **bold** is emphasis. Instructions in `[brackets]` are presenter actions and should not be spoken.

---

## 1. Opening — AI Transformation, AI-Native by Design

**Timing: 1–1.5 minutes**

[Start on the hero section. Pause for two seconds before speaking.]

Good morning, and thank you for the opportunity to introduce 01.AI.

Most organizations have already started adopting AI. They have models, copilots, experiments and a growing number of proofs of concept.

But adoption is not the same as transformation.

The real question is not, “How many AI tools have we deployed?” The real question is: **Has AI changed how the organization understands what is happening, makes consequential decisions, coordinates execution and improves operating results?**

That is the transition from AI adoption to an **AI-native organization**.

At 01.AI, we work with CEOs, governments and industry leaders to make that transition real. We connect enterprise context, decision intelligence and physical operations into one governed operating system.

[Point to the operating-system visual.]

On the left are executive decisions, organizational workflows and dynamic enterprise context. On the right are assets, digital twins, robots, drones and field operations.

The center is the same operating loop:

**Context. Decision. Action. Verification.**

That is the architecture I will show you today.

## 3. What We Provide — Product, Platform and Responsibility

**Timing: 1.5–2 minutes**

[Scroll to “Products, platforms and responsibility.”]

Our offer has four connected layers.

**TrueNorth** is our decision-intelligence product family. It supports executive decisions, investment decisions, commercial decisions and role-specific decisions across the organization.

**WorldWise** is our enterprise agent platform. It provides multi-model access, agent orchestration, tool use, logging, policy and governance. We remain model-neutral because enterprise customers need the freedom to use the best available models without rebuilding the operating system around each one.

The third layer is **AI transformation and design**. Before we build, we identify the decisions and workflows that matter, define the required context, design governance and establish the value metrics.

The fourth layer is **Forward-Deployed Engineering**, or FDE.

Our FDE teams work inside the real operating environment. They do not stop at a recommendation or a demonstration. They integrate systems, redesign workflows, manage exceptions, measure results and transfer capability to the client.

The principle is simple: **services must create product learning and reusable assets. Otherwise AI delivery becomes low-margin custom work rather than a scalable capability.**

## 4. Proof — Different Industries, One Operating Method

**Timing: 1.5 minutes**

[Scroll through the three case examples.]

Let me show three different operating realities.

In mining and resources, the problem is not a lack of dashboards. It is the coordination of mine, rail, port, inventory, equipment, weather and maintenance constraints. The value comes from improving real operational decisions across the integrated supply chain.

In agriculture, multiple data sources—video, IoT, environmental and production data—must feed a decision engine that supports farming, inventory, logistics and industrial operations.

And at the sovereign level, national AI capability is not a single model. It requires infrastructure, governance, education, multi-industry applications, local control and a sustainable ecosystem.

These cases look different, but the method is consistent:

**Start with a consequential decision or workflow. Build the authoritative context. Enter production. Prove the outcome. Then scale.**

## 5. Two Operating Realities — Decision Intelligence and Physical Intelligence

**Timing: 1.5 minutes**

[Scroll to “AI must operate in two worlds.”]

AI must operate in two worlds.

The first is the digital and organizational world.

Here, Decision Intelligence helps executives and teams answer questions such as:

What changed? What deserves management attention? Where is the evidence incomplete or conflicting? What action is required? Who owns it? By when? And did the action change the result?

The second is the physical world.

Here, Physical Intelligence must understand assets, locations, sensors, operating history, constraints and safety boundaries. It may use perception, simulation, drones, robots and other equipment—but always within governed human authority.

These are not two unrelated product lines. They share the same architecture:

**Ontology as the map. Dynamic context as the GPS. Reasoning as the brain. Governed action as the operating system.**

## 6. Ontology — The Computable Map of the Institution

**Timing: 1.5 minutes**

[Scroll to Ontology. If appropriate, click “Open interactive ontology.”]

A general-purpose model understands language. It does not automatically understand your institution.

It does not know which asset is connected to which facility, which organization owns which obligation, which measurement came from which source, or which decision depends on which evidence.

Ontology makes that world computable.

It converts people, assets, organizations, policies, events, measurements and relationships into governed business objects.

[Point to industry domains and graph relationships.]

This is not just a knowledge graph. Provenance and lineage show where a fact came from, what changed, how confident we are and what downstream decisions may be affected.

This is how AI moves from generating plausible language to operating with enterprise context.

## 7. Leadership AI — From Operating Truth to Accountable Action

**Timing: 3–4 minutes**

[Scroll to the embedded Leadership AI workspace.]

This is Leadership AI.

It is not another executive dashboard, and it is not a chatbot waiting for the CEO to ask the perfect question.

Its purpose is to manage the complete decision lifecycle: identify what deserves leadership attention, assemble evidence, expose conflicts, form a bounded judgment, assign action and verify the result.

### Daily Vanguard

[Start on Daily Vanguard.]

The Daily Vanguard is the executive entry point. It combines scheduled reporting with event-driven alerts.

The system is not presenting every available metric. It is selecting the issues where leadership attention can materially change the outcome.

[Click the National Logistics Corridor risk alert.]

Here, the system identifies a conflict between a major funding request and verified delivery progress.

The important point is not the alert itself. The system organizes the evidence, distinguishes confirmed facts from assumptions and shows the impact chain.

This gives the executive a decision-ready view—not merely more information.

### Execution Tracking

[Click “View execution detail,” or use Execution Tracking in the sidebar.]

Once a decision is made, it enters the execution system.

We can see strategic alignment, ownership, status, evidence, milestones and intervention recommendations.

This closes a common enterprise gap: the gap between management discussion and accountable execution.

### Smart Meetings

[Click Smart Meetings.]

Smart Meetings prepare the executive before the meeting, organize the relevant evidence during the meeting, and convert decisions into commitments afterward.

The objective is not to produce longer meeting summaries. It is to make the meeting part of the operating system.

### Strategic Analysis

[Click Strategic Analysis.]

For strategic questions, the system makes alternatives explicit.

Here we can compare a full funding release, a conditional staged release and a pause-and-replan option. Each option has a different delivery impact, budget exposure and dependency structure.

AI can recommend an option, but the decision boundary remains clear. The executive retains authority, and the system preserves the evidence and reasoning behind the choice.

That is what we mean by governed decision intelligence.

## 8. Physical Intelligence — Three Levels of Reality

**Timing: 4–5 minutes**

[Scroll to Physical Intelligence Demos.]

Now let us move from organizational decisions into physical operations.

### National Energy Digital Twin

[Launch Energy System Intelligence.]

At the national level, the system moves from global energy context to national resources, export corridors, domestic operations and the AI infrastructure layer.

[Scroll through two or three acts; do not explain every number.]

The purpose is not simply to visualize the country. The digital twin creates a live operating state that can support scenario analysis, policy decisions and cross-system coordination.

A digital twin shows state. A **decision twin** connects that state to judgment, simulation, action and verified outcomes.

[Exit the demo.]

### Refinery Live Digital Twin

[Launch the refinery demo.]

At the industrial-site level, the process begins with a whole-site scan.

Multiple agents review inventory, dispatch and operating context. Explainable anomalies are downgraded. The remaining unexplained anomaly receives deeper attention.

[Open the TK-104 verification if it is not already visible.]

This is a critical design principle: AI should not simply create more alarms. It should reduce noise, organize evidence and focus human attention on the few issues that require intervention.

[Exit the demo.]

### Solar Embodied AI Inspection

[Launch the Solar Inspection demo.]

This is the complete embodied-AI operating loop.

We have a 160-panel solar field, five drones and five Go2 ground robots. The interface includes live telemetry, FPV views, task orchestration, panel monitoring, safety controls and natural-language mission control.

[Open AI Command, or issue the command through the appropriate command interface.]

The mission command is:

**“Inspect solar field.”**

[Wait for the mission to start.]

Five drones divide the aerial inspection. They scan the field and identify suspected anomalies.

The system does not treat an aerial detection as a confirmed defect. It hands the anomaly to a Go2 ground robot for closer verification.

[Point to Active Tasks, Faults, Fleet and Task Log.]

You can see the mission state, active task, detected panels, fleet status and the command trace.

This is the proof unit for Physical Intelligence:

**Mission command → aerial scan → anomaly detection → ground verification → operational action.**

And the safety boundary remains explicit. AI plans and coordinates the mission; equipment executes within defined constraints; human operators retain authority and exception control.

[Exit the demo.]

## 9. Engagement — Start With One Consequential Loop

**Timing: 1 minute**

[Scroll to the final section.]

The mistake many organizations make is trying to rebuild the entire enterprise before proving value.

We take the opposite approach.

Start with one consequential loop: one executive decision or one physical operation where better context, faster judgment and stronger execution can produce a measurable result.

Then we align the sponsor, diagnose the workflow, model the context, build the lighthouse, enter production, verify the outcome and scale what works.

The first engagement should answer five questions:

Is there a real executive sponsor?

Is the problem connected to an operating or financial outcome?

Can AI enter the actual workflow rather than sit beside it?

Can we define evidence and acceptance before the build begins?

And can the result become a reusable capability rather than a one-off project?

**Timing: 45–60 seconds**

[Return to the hero or remain on the engagement section.]

Let me close with one thought.

The next phase of AI will not be won by the organization with the largest number of pilots.

It will be won by the organization that can turn its reality into computable context, its judgment into governed decisions, and its decisions into verified action.

That is what it means to become AI-native.

01.AI brings together the strategy, products, ontology, agents, physical intelligence and forward-deployed delivery required to make that transition real.

We do not begin by asking which model you want.

We begin by asking:

**Which decision or operation is important enough to transform—and how will we prove that it worked?**

Thank you.
